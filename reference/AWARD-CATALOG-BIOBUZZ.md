## 0. TL;DR — the 14 things that matter

1. **The entire A-rule set A201-A215 is evergreen** — all 15 carry the leading asterisk. [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ] Per §1.7.1 *This Document & Its Conventions*, evergreen means "the overall subject and presence of the rule from season to season is constant, but game specific details and terms may be updated," and evergreen rules "start their respective section, so their rule number is less likely to change." Practical effect: **A201-A215 numbering is safe to memorize and cite all season.**
2. **"Structured Interview" is dead. It is now the "Initial Interview"** and it may be **scheduled in-person, unscheduled in the pits, or remote** — the Event Director picks the format and must tell teams before the event. [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1.2] Prepare a presentation that survives a noisy pit.
3. **Judges no longer receive Event Volunteer Feedback.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ Figure 6-2] DECODE's Figure 6-2 listed four information sources including "Event Volunteer Feedback"; BIOBUZZ lists exactly three — Initial Interview, Follow-up Interview(s), PORTFOLIO. Deliberations are now a closed set. What you did not say or write does not exist.
4. **PII went from "encouraged" to mandatory.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ A201] "Teams **must** strictly minimize Personally Identifying Information (PII) in the PORTFOLIO. For student privacy, use only first names and last initials. While student photographs are permitted, full names must not be disclosed." DECODE said teams were "encouraged to limit" PII. This is now a compliance item, not a best practice.
5. **AI is explicitly allowed, with a credit.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ A201] "Teams may use AI and research aids to compose their portfolios, provided they respect intellectual property rights and include a footnote or endnote credit. Example Credit: 'Portfolio created by Team XXXXX and ChatGPT'". **This entire harness is legal. Put the footnote in.**
6. **Judges will not click anything.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ A201] "JUDGES will not click on links, websites, or videos in a PORTFOLIO. They also cannot take extra printed papers from an interview back to their judging room. Teams should put everything they want JUDGES to see directly in their PORTFOLIO." QR codes, Drive links and a slick website are worth zero points.
7. **Think Award got much narrower and much cheaper to win.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.3.2] DECODE had three criteria including a whole "resources / mentors / recruiting / goal-tracking" block. BIOBUZZ has **two**: (1) a PORTFOLIO containing at least one of four engineering topics, (2) encouraged — "easy to read and easy to find." It is now purely an engineering-documentation award with no TA overlap.
8. **Connect / Reach / Sustain were rewritten in "competency" language** with harder, more specific bars. [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.3.3-6.3.5] Reach in particular now requires **all three** of objectives, strategy, and how the activities grow the FIRST community.
9. **Section 6.6 Project-Based Global Awards is a placeholder.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ] DECODE named the Digital Animation Award (WPI) and the Safety Animation Award (UL Solutions); BIOBUZZ V0 names none — "More information about Project-Based Global Awards coming soon!" Section 4.1.4 still cites "Safety Animation Award" as an example, so they are expected back.
10. **The FIRST Leadership Award grade-eligibility note was deleted.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.5.1] DECODE stated the award targets students "two (2) to three (3) years away from entering college or university." BIOBUZZ V0 removes that note entirely and points only at the webpage. **Do not assume the old eligibility window** — verify on the FIRST Leadership Award page before nominating.
11. **New Inspire escape hatches.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ A213, A214] FIRST Championship and FIRST Premier Events are now an explicit exception to the HOME REGION restriction, and a team that won 1st Place Inspire at a Qualifying/League Tournament "**are eligible to win it at their Regional Championship**."
12. **A215 is per-team, and this program has two teams.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ] "Teams are only eligible to win or be a runner-up for a **single team judged award** at the event." The A team and the B team are separately registered teams, so the program's ceiling at one event is **two judged awards**, not one. New note in BIOBUZZ: A215 "does not prevent a team from winning additional awards listed in Section 6.4 Tournament ALLIANCE Awards and Section 6.5 Individual Awards."
13. **Inspire is worth 5x everything else.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ Table 4-1] 60 / 30 / 15 advancement points for Inspire 1st/2nd/3rd, versus 12 / 6 / 3 for every other points-eligible award. A 2nd place Inspire (30) beats 1st place in two other awards (24) — which one team cannot do anyway under A215.
14. **A robot is optional for judging.** [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1, A204 note] "Teams may participate in judging regardless of the inspection status of their ROBOT and are eligible for awards even if they are attending the event without a ROBOT." Judging is the one scoring surface a broken robot cannot take away.

## 2. Full A-rule inventory (A201-A215)

**All 15 rules are evergreen** (leading asterisk on the headline). **No other A-rules exist in BIOBUZZ V0** — verified by grep across the full layout extraction; every other `A2xx` occurrence is a cross-reference.

These rules also start their respective section, so their rule number is less likely to change from season to season."

## 4. The judged-award machinery

### 4.1 The Initial Interview [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1.2]

**Renamed from DECODE's "Structured Interview."** Three legal formats, chosen by the Event Director "in coordination with the JUDGE Advisor and the regional planning committee," communicated to teams before the event, and applied uniformly: "All teams, regardless of the option chosen, are expected to be interviewed using the same format at their event."

| Format | Description (verbatim) | What changes for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Scheduled in-person** | "A planned meeting with JUDGES, at a specific time, in a dedicated judging space" | The classic. You get a known slot and a quiet room |
| **Unscheduled in-person** | "The interview is not scheduled for a specific time. JUDGES meet teams in their pit area at the event or in a dedicated judging space" | **New format.** You must be interview-ready from the moment doors open. Assume the noisy version |
| **Remote** | "A meeting with the JUDGES held online on a video conferencing platform" | Held before in-person gameplay in hybrid events |

"All three types of Initial Interviews have the same rules and allow teams to share information with the JUDGES."

**Pit-format warning, verbatim:** "Teams participating at events where the Initial Interview is conducted in the pits should be mindful that the pit area is an active and often noisy environment... external distractions (such as pit announcements and general background noise) may occur during the interview and could create communication challenges." FIRST also commits that "JUDGES will work with MATCH queuers and technical volunteers to ensure teams are able to attend MATCHES and work on their ROBOTS."

#### Interview timeline [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ Figure 6-3, read from the rendered figure]

```
 1. JUDGE          2. Presentation    3. Q&A          4. Interview    5. JUDGES    6. Next
    Introduction      5 Mins             5 Mins          Concludes       Discuss      Team
                      or less            or more
```

Numbered process, verbatim:

1. "JUDGES introduce themselves to the team and ask if the team has a prepared presentation they would like to start with."
2. "Teams may present to the JUDGES uninterrupted for up to about 5 minutes, per A205." *(note: the prose cites A205; the 5-minute reservation is actually A207)*
3. "JUDGES will ask open ended questions and interact with the team for the remaining interview time."
4. "The interview is concluded by the JUDGES."
5. "JUDGES privately discuss the details of the interview and complete the feedback form (coming soon)."
6. "JUDGES identify the next team to be interviewed and repeat the process."

#### The two mandatory questions [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1.2]

"At each event, the JUDGE Advisor will select two questions from the question bank that all teams will be asked at the start of the Initial Interview's question and answer session. **One question will be focused on the MCI award category, and one question will be focused on the TA award category.**" After those two, JUDGES may ask anything.

- Source: the **Judge Interview Question Bank** — marked **(coming soon)** in V0. **Watch for its release.**
- FIRST warns: "Additional questions asked during interviews may come from the question bank but teams should be prepared to answer questions that are not listed within the document."

**[JUDGMENT]** Two of your Q&A questions are known in advance by category. Every student should be able to answer one MCI question and one TA question cold. That is the cheapest possible interview preparation and it is guaranteed to be tested.

### 4.2 Pit interviews (follow-up) [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1.3]

"After all the judging panels have finished the Initial Interviews, the JUDGES compare notes and may elect to follow up with the teams in the pit area." Teams may "expand upon materials presented in the Initial Interview and share additional content with the JUDGES (e.g., ROBOT prototypes, design artifacts, and photos or letters from outreach events). A team does not need to prepare another presentation for this pit interview but should be ready to answer questions."

**The catch, verbatim:** "JUDGES may read additional information during pit interviews but **will not bring back additional content to be referenced as part of the JUDGE deliberations.**"

**[JUDGMENT]** A pit visit is a strong signal you are in contention. Content shown there influences a judge's memory but cannot be entered into evidence. Anything that must survive deliberation has to be inside the 15 pages.

### 4.3 What JUDGES may and may not consider [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1.1]

**Permitted — exactly three sources** (Figure 6-2): Initial Interview · Follow-up Interview(s) · PORTFOLIO (where applicable).

**Season boundary:** "For the purposes of judging, the current season begins on **January 1, 2026**. JUDGES cannot consider information from outside of what they have seen or heard at the current event."

**Explicitly disallowed, verbatim list:**

- "Past performance (good or bad) of a team,"
- "Personal knowledge of a team,"
- "External sources such as websites and/or social media,"
- "A ROBOT's performance (e.g., how many points scored in a MATCH) unless it is specifically listed as an award criteria,"
- "ROBOT penalties during gameplay, or"
- "A team's ranking in the tournament."

**[JUDGMENT]** Two of those six directly benefit a small program: your match record and your ranking are firewalled out of the judging room. A team that goes 2-4 on the field and interviews brilliantly is not penalized for the record.

### 4.4 Outreach quality standard [CONFIRMED-BIOBUZZ §6.1.4]

"In general, JUDGES will consider ongoing, sustained outreach to be of higher quality than occasional or one-off outreach. JUDGES will seek to understand what is the impact of the outreach to the individuals being reached."

Teams are directed to the **Outreach Terms and Definitions Document** for definitions behind claims like "starting a FIRST team," "running an event," "reaching x number of people," and warned: "JUDGES may ask specific questions when a specific term listed in this document is mentioned in a team's PORTFOLIO or during an interview." **Do not use a defined term you cannot defend against its official definition.**

## 6. Winnability ranking for a ~15-student, two-team, modest-budget program

**[JUDGMENT throughout this section.]** Calibrated to: ~15 students across an A team and a B team, modest budget, limited fabrication capability, limited mentor hours.

### 6.1 The design-dependent vs team-dependent split

This is the hinge for downstream award-alignment work.

| Award | Dependency | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Innovate** | 🔧 **DESIGN-DEPENDENT** | Won because a specific mechanism is creative/unique and works. Criteria R2 and R3 are statements about the machine. Design choice determines eligibility |
| **Control** | 🔧 **DESIGN-DEPENDENT** (software-weighted) | R2 requires "external feedback to control the ROBOT." Won because of what the control system *is*. But the fabrication cost is near zero — this is the design-dependent award a low-fab program can actually reach |
| **Design** | 🔧 **DESIGN-DEPENDENT** (whole-robot) | R2 explicitly requires "the design of the **entire** ROBOT, or the detailed process used to create it." Cannot be won with one good mechanism |
| **Think** | 👥 **TEAM-DEPENDENT** (documentation) | Won because of how the team records its work. The robot supplies the subject matter, but the *quality of the writing* decides it. A mediocre robot with excellent documentation wins Think |
| **Connect** | 👥 **TEAM-DEPENDENT** | Pure team-operations award. Robot is irrelevant to every criterion |
| **Reach** | 👥 **TEAM-DEPENDENT** | Pure outreach/recruiting. Robot is irrelevant |
| **Sustain** | 👥 **TEAM-DEPENDENT** | Pure organizational practice. Robot is irrelevant |
| **Judges' Choice** | 👥 **TEAM-DEPENDENT** | "unique efforts, performance, or dynamics" — about the team as observed |
| **Inspire** | 🔧👥 **BOTH, simultaneously** | R2 requires strong contention in MCI **and** TA **and** Think. Cannot be won on one axis |
| **Winning / Finalist Alliance** | 🔧 **PERFORMANCE-DEPENDENT** | Not judged at all. Determined by match play and alliance selection |
| **Compass / FIRST Leadership** | 👥 **TEAM-DEPENDENT** (individual) | About a mentor / a student, not the machine |

**Load-bearing consequence:** of the eight named team judged awards, **five are team-dependent and three are design-dependent.** For a program with limited fabrication capability, five of eight awards are reachable without touching the machine — and one of the three design-dependent ones (Control) is reachable with sensors and code rather than a mill.

### 6.2 Ranked winnability

| Rank | Award | Dep. | Verdict | Reasoning |
|:---:|---|:---:|---|---|
| **1** | **Think** | 👥 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Highest ROI** | Two criteria total, one required. Zero fabrication cost. The BIOBUZZ rewrite stripped out the TA-overlap criterion, so it is now the single most tractable award in the catalog. R1 can be satisfied by option (C) alone — a table of ideas considered and why you picked one. **It is also a hard prerequisite for Inspire.** Both teams should build a portfolio; this is the award the B team should openly target |
| **2** | **Control** | 🔧 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Best design-dependent option** | The one MCI award that rewards code and sensors over machining. R2 needs *one* external-feedback solution — an encoder-based drive, a distance sensor that gates an action, a closed-loop arm. Cheap in dollars, expensive only in student hours, and students are the resource you have. Requires a PORTFOLIO section (A/B/C), which you are writing anyway for Think |
| **3** | **Sustain** | 👥 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Structurally favored** | **Running two teams from ~15 students on a modest budget is literally the award's subject matter.** E3 (defined roles, "intentional process for preparing future student leaders") is what an A/B team structure *is* — B team is the pipeline. E4 (constraints, mitigation, adapting plans) is what a modest budget forces you to do anyway. You are not manufacturing a story; you are writing down the one you already have |
| **4** | **Connect** | 👥 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Cheap required criterion** | R1 is the *only* required criterion and it is a document: learning goals + steps to reach them. That can be written in an afternoon. E2/E3 need real STEM professional relationships, which cost mentor hours — your scarcest resource. Win condition: two or three genuine, recurring professional contacts beat a list of twenty one-off emails, per §6.1.4 |
| **5** | **Innovate** | 🔧 | ⭐⭐⭐ **Reachable if narrowly scoped** | The award text explicitly permits "one MECHANISM on the ROBOT." A small team can win by picking **one** subsystem, iterating it visibly, and documenting the path. The gate is R3: "stable and reliable... most of the time" — clever but flaky loses. Requires prototypes/CAD/sketches as evidence (R1), so photograph every iteration from week one |
| **6** | **Reach** | 👥 | ⭐⭐⭐ **Doable but front-loaded** | R2 is a hard, verifiable outcome: recruit new teams, coaches, mentors, or volunteers "who have not previously participated in the FIRST community." That takes a season-long campaign started early, not an event-week scramble. R1's three-part structure (objectives / strategy / growth of FIRST) is newly strict. Caution: JUDGES may test your claims against the Outreach Terms and Definitions Document |
| **7** | **Compass** | 👥 | ⭐⭐⭐ **Trivially cheap, gated on advancement** | A 60-second video. Costs one evening. But it is "offered at the Regional Championship tournament level" — you must advance first (or reach FIRST Championship). Zero advancement points, but it costs nothing and honors a mentor whose hours are the program's constraint. Watch the format rules: no streaming links, ≤60 s including credits, music must be licensed and credited |
| **8** | **Judges' Choice** | 👥 | ⭐⭐ **Cannot be targeted** | No published criteria, discretionary at every event size. Treat as upside, never as a plan |
| **9** | **FIRST Leadership Award** | 👥 | ⭐⭐ **Low cost, off-manual, zero points** | One student nomination through a separate FIRST process. **BIOBUZZ deleted the eligibility note**, so confirm the grade window on the webpage before spending effort. No advancement value |
| **10** | **Design** | 🔧 | ⭐⭐ **Requires fabrication capability** | R2 forces whole-robot consideration — "not just one COMPONENT" — and E3 rewards a robot that "stands out... because of its appearance and how well it functions." Appearance and whole-robot coherence are exactly what a CNC-equipped program buys. BIOBUZZ softened R1 to "and/or" (so "practical to maintain" alone satisfies it), which helps, but R2 remains the wall. **Realistic path if any: win on "the detailed process used to create it" rather than on the finished machine** |
| **11** | **Inspire** | 🔧👥 | ⭐ **Stretch goal, multi-season** | R2 requires being a strong contender in MCI **and** TA **and** Think **simultaneously**, in one interview and 15 pages, against teams doing the same with 30 students. Worth 60 points — 5x anything else — and gated by A213 (HOME REGION only) and A214 (once per season from QT/LT). **Plan for it as a 2-3 season objective, not a season-one target.** The BIOBUZZ A214 note gives a genuine second chance at RCMP |
| **12** | **Project-Based Global** | — | ❓ **Unknown** | Deferred in V0. [HISTORICAL] the DECODE versions were animation awards, requiring an animator. Zero advancement points. Revisit when FIRST publishes |

### 6.3 Which awards effectively require a large program

| Award | Why scale wins | Can a small program beat it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | Whole-robot fit and finish, aesthetic distinctiveness, and rapid maintainability are downstream of fabrication capability, spare-part budget, and iteration count | Only via the "detailed process" clause in R2 — beat them on documented process, not on the object |
| **Inspire** | Requires simultaneous depth in three categories, which is a headcount problem: you need parallel sub-teams for robot, outreach, and documentation | Yes, but not in one season, and it requires the two-team structure to be a genuine strength story rather than a resource split |
| **Reach** at 2nd/3rd-place depth | Outreach volume scales directly with student count and mentor hours | R2 rewards *successful recruitment*, not volume. One new team started beats fifty demo-day handshakes |
| **Stacked places at 41-64 team events** | More teams means deeper podiums but a far stronger field | Table 6-1 shows the discretionary 2nd/3rd places are where mid-tier teams get on the board — do not skip large events assuming you cannot place |

### 6.4 Recommended two-team split [JUDGMENT]

A215 gives you two independent award ceilings. Wasting them by having both teams present the same story is the most avoidable error available.

| | A team | B team |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary target** | Control (design-dependent) or Innovate | **Think** (team-dependent, documentation) |
| **Secondary target** | Design or Innovate | Sustain or Connect |
| **Portfolio emphasis** | Control system contents per Table 6-8 R1 (A/B/C) + engineering process | Engineering journey narrative + readability (Table 6-3 R1 + E2) |
| **Rationale** | Puts the robot-heavy team where machine criteria live | Puts the developing team where fabrication capability is irrelevant and where BIOBUZZ *lowered* the bar this season |
| **Shared** | Both must satisfy A203 (interview) and A201/A202 (portfolio spec). Sustain evidence — the A/B structure itself — is shared material either can use | |
