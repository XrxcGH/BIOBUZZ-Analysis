# AWARD ALIGNMENT MATRIX — BIOBUZZ 2026-27

### From a chosen design/scoring strategy → the ~2 awards to target → the materials to build

**Scope.** This file is the *pairing stage* of the harness. Upstream, `reference/ACHIEVABILITY-FACTORS.md`
ranks candidate strategies; `reference/ROBOT-ARCHETYPE-LIBRARY.md` names the shapes those strategies take.
This file answers the next question: **given that we are building THAT robot, which two awards does the build
naturally generate evidence for, and what exactly must we produce to make that evidence judgeable?**

**Calibration target.** ~15 students across two registered FTC teams (A and B), each fielding its own robot.
Modest budget, hand tools + 3D printing (no CNC mill), limited mentor hours.

**Status on 2026-08-21.** Section 6 (Awards, A-rules) is **FINAL for BIOBUZZ**. Every award name, every
criterion, and every A-rule below is real 2026-27 text read from
`manuals/2026-27_BIOBUZZ/sections/06_Awards_A_p43-58.txt`. What is **not** knowable until Kickoff
(2026-09-12) is which archetype the game rewards — so the rows are game-agnostic and the columns are exact.

> **[C] The single most important structural fact on this page.** Only **three** of the eight named team
> judged awards — **Innovate, Control, Design** (the MCI category) — are moved by what you build. The other
> five are moved by how the team operates. **No robot decision can win Connect, Reach, or Sustain, and no
> robot decision can win Inspire by itself** (Table 6-2 R2 requires being a strong contender in an MCI award
> **and** a TA award **and** Think, simultaneously). Everything in §2-§4 of this file is therefore a plan for
> *one MCI slot plus Think* — the TA leg runs on a separate track and is not optional if Inspire is the goal.

Additional judging mechanics that shape materials **[C]**:
- **§6.1.1** — judges may consider **only** the Initial Interview, any follow-up interview, and the PORTFOLIO,
  and **only** what they saw or heard **at the current event**. Explicitly excluded: past performance, personal
  knowledge of a team, external websites/social media, **a robot's match points unless an award criterion says
  so**, penalties, and tournament ranking. → *Match performance is not evidence. Measured performance written
  into your portfolio is.*
- **§6.1.2** — the JUDGE Advisor picks **two questions from the Judge Interview Question Bank** that every team
  is asked at the start of Q&A: **one MCI question and one TA question**. → *Rehearse one of each.*
- **§6.1.3** — pit interviews let you show **prototypes, design artifacts, photos/letters** that are not in the
  portfolio, but judges **will not carry additional content back** to deliberations. → *Pit material persuades
  the judge in front of you; only the portfolio persuades the room.*
- **A212*** — every team gets a feedback form from the Initial Interview (initial impression only, not used in
  deliberations). → *Free calibration data between events. See §5.4.*
- **§6.1** — **no awards are presented at League Meets.** League Tournaments do have judging.

## 1.3 Design-dependent vs team-dependent, restated as a planning rule [J]

```
        WHAT YOU BUILD                    HOW YOU OPERATE
   +----------------------+        +-----------------------------+
   | Innovate | Control   |        | Connect | Reach | Sustain   |
   |        Design        |        |     (+ Judges' Choice)      |
   +----------+-----------+        +--------------+--------------+
              |                                   |
              +------------+   THINK   +----------+
                           |  (bridge) |
                           +-----+-----+
                                 |
                              INSPIRE   <- needs all three legs (Table 6-2 R2)
```

**Think is the bridge.** It is the only award whose subject is the robot but whose criteria are entirely about
*how you recorded the work* (Table 6-3 R1 A/B/C/D). That is why Think appears as the hedge in most pairings
below: it is the cheapest award in the catalog for this program (`AWARD-CATALOG-BIOBUZZ.md` §6.2 ranks it #1),
and it is a hard prerequisite for Inspire.

# 3. Materials: the six packages

Rather than repeat the same instructions sixteen times, the materials are defined once as **packages M1-M6**.
Each pairing card in §4 names its packages and then adds archetype-specific deltas.

**Every package obeys A201:** everything lands inside the 15 content pages, US Letter or A4, ≥10 pt type, no
links or QR codes, first names + last initials only, dated on or after 2026-01-01, AI credited (§7).

## M1 — Simplicity & coherence package → **Design Award (6.3.8)**

**Argument being made:** *this robot is elegant, efficient and maintainable, it was designed that way on
purpose, and the whole machine — not one part — was designed as a system that serves our match strategy.*

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Portfolio pages** | **1 p — Whole-robot overview**: labelled robot photo + exploded diagram, mass and motor budget, and one sentence per subsystem stating the job it does (Design **R2**, entire robot). **1 p — Design rationale**: the strategy statement, then each major choice traced back to it (Design **E4**, **E5**). **½ p — Maintainability**: measured module-swap times, tool list, spares kit contents (Design **R1** "practical to maintain") |
| **Data to collect during build** | Module-swap stopwatch times (target: name the number, e.g. "intake off the robot in 90 s with 2 tools"); fastener/tool count; part-count reduction across revisions; dated whole-robot photos at each major revision (aim: one per month from first assembly); a CAD or hand-drawn revision index with dates and the reason for each revision |
| **Pit demo** | The **teardown demo**: two students remove and reinstall one module in front of the judge, on the clock. This is the single most persuasive Design-award pit artifact available to a small team and it costs nothing |
| **Judge talking points** | (1) "We had *N* subsystems and cut to *M* — here is the one we deleted and why." (2) "Any student on either team can swap this module in under *X* seconds; here is the tool kit." (3) "The design follows from our match strategy: we chose *one repeated action* and built the whole robot to do it faster." (4) Name the trade you **lost** by staying simple — judges reward the honest version |
| **Separate submission** | **None.** Design does not require a PORTFOLIO (6.3.8) — it can be won on the interview and pit alone. Build the pages anyway: they are shared with Think and Inspire |

## M2 — One-mechanism creativity package → **Innovate Award sponsored by RTX (6.3.6)**

**Argument being made:** *this specific mechanism is creative or unique, we can show the path that produced
it, it works most of the time, and here is how we reduced the risk it carried.*

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Portfolio pages** | **1 p — The mechanism**: what problem it solves, what it is, annotated photo + CAD render. **1 p — Development path**: 3-5 dated prototype images in order, each with the failure that produced the next one (Innovate **R1** "examples of their engineering work"). **½ p — Risk reduction**: the risk register — what could go wrong, what you changed, what the residual risk is (Innovate **E4**). **½ p — Works most of the time**: the success-rate table (Innovate **R3**) |
| **Data to collect during build** | Photograph **every** prototype the day it exists, in the same lighting, with a ruler or scale object in frame; keep the *rejected* prototypes physically — they are the best pit artifact in FTC; log attempt/success counts for the mechanism from the first working day (see M5); record the load test if it carries load (mass, factor of safety, what failed first) |
| **Pit demo** | The mechanism operated **in isolation**, on the bench or on the robot, plus the **shelf of rejected prototypes** in version order. Let the judge hold v1 and v4 |
| **Judge talking points** | (1) "Here is version 1, here is what broke, here is version 4." (2) "It is unique because *X* — the common approach is *Y*, and we chose differently because *Z*." (3) "It works *N* times out of *M* across our last *K* matches" — 6.3.6 says the design "does not need to work every time," so give the honest rate. (4) "The risk was *R*; we mitigated it by *M*; the residual risk is *r* and here is the abort behaviour" |
| **Separate submission** | **None.** Innovate does not require a PORTFOLIO (6.3.6) |
| **Hard gate [C]** | **R3 is Required: the creative design "must be stable and reliable" and help reach game goals "most of the time."** Do not pair Innovate with a mechanism you cannot demonstrate. A clever mechanism that fails in the pit loses this award outright |

## M3 — Sensors & feedback package → **Control Award (6.3.7)**

**Argument being made:** *these are our control components, this is the challenge each one solves, this is how
each works, at least one of them uses external feedback, and here is the measured evidence that it works.*

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Portfolio pages** | **2 p, mandatory** — this is the only award whose portfolio contents are dictated by the criterion. **Page 1: the control-system block diagram** — every sensor and actuator, what it is wired to, and the signal flow. **Page 2: the component table**, one row per component with three columns matching Table 6-8 R1 exactly: *component* (A) · *challenge it solves* (B) · *how it functions* (C). Then a boxed callout naming the **external-feedback loop(s)** (R2). **½ p — Reliability**: measured success rate per behaviour, before/after a tuning change (E3, E4). **½ p — What we learned** using the engineering process on the control system (E5) |
| **Data to collect during build** | One dated experiment file per control change: *what we measured, the numbers before, the change, the numbers after*. Tuning curves (PID/feedforward constant vs. measured error or settling time); AUTO success rate by day, by field, by venue lighting; sensor failure log (when it misread, and what the fallback did); encoder-vs-actual distance calibration table; camera exposure/lighting notes if vision is used |
| **Pit demo** | Run the closed loop **while the judge watches it correct**: push the arm off target by hand and let it return; occlude the sensor and let the fallback fire; drive to a taped mark twice from different starts. **Seeing the correction happen is worth more than any diagram** |
| **Judge talking points** | (1) "Our external feedback is *sensor X* closing the loop on *actuator Y*; without it *this* fails." (2) "It succeeds *N/M*; here is the failure mode and here is the fallback branch." (3) "Here is the tuning curve — we changed *constant* from *a* to *b* because *measurement*." (4) "The code is not in the portfolio because 6.3.7 says it does not need to be; the block diagram is on page *p*" |
| **Separate submission** | **PORTFOLIO is REQUIRED [C] Table 6-8 R1.** Missing the A202 submission deadline makes Control ineligible regardless of how good the robot is |
| **Hard gate [C]** | **R2 requires *external* feedback.** A timed open-loop routine, a `sleep()`, or a preset motor power is **not** external feedback. An encoder, limit switch, distance sensor, IMU heading hold, colour sensor gate, or camera all are |

## M4 — Process & comparison package → **Think Award (6.3.2)**

**Argument being made:** *here is a clear, organised, readable record of the engineering journey — with at
least one of: the process, lessons applied, alternatives compared, or math that decided something.*

Table 6-3 R1 requires only **one** of A/B/C/D. **[J] Produce C and D anyway — they are the two most students
skip and the two judges ask about.**

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Portfolio pages** | **1 p — Decision matrix (R1.C)**: one honest table, options across the top, weighted criteria down the side, the winner **and the rejected option named**, with a sentence on what the rejection cost. **1 p — The math (R1.D)**: one real calculation that *changed* a decision — gear ratio, torque budget, cycle-time model, tolerance stack, battery-current budget. # 6. Two-team split guidance

## 6.1 The mechanics [C]

- **A215** caps each team at **one** judged award (or runner-up) per event. **The cap is per team** — two
  registered teams give the organization **two independent award slots**. No anti-sweep rule exists in Section 6.
- **A213 / A214** (Inspire region limit; 1st-place Inspire once per season from QT/LT) are also **per team** —
  an A-team Inspire win does not affect the B team.
- **A211 Table 6-1** is the real constraint: at a **4-10 team** event only **one** MCI award and **one** TA
  award are given at all, so **your two teams may be competing against each other for a single slot**. At
  **21-40 teams** the pool includes 2nd places across most categories. **[J] Your joint ceiling is far higher
  at a mid-size event than a tiny one.**
- **§6.1.1** bars judges from using "personal knowledge of a team" or anything outside the current event. That
  protects the B team from being marked down as new — and means **the B team gets zero credit for anything it
  does not say in its own interview and its own portfolio.**
- **A203/A204.A/A207/A208** all apply per team: two interviews, two prepared presentations, ≥2 students each,
  and **two available adults** if the slots collide. See `research/TWO-TEAM-PROGRAM-RULES.md` §5.3.

## 6.2 The recommended split

Consistent with `AWARD-CATALOG-BIOBUZZ.md` §6.4 and `ROBOT-ARCHETYPE-LIBRARY.md` §5.3.

| | **A team** | **B team** |
|---|---|---|
| Robot identity | Specialist in the season's **highest-value repeatable task** | Specialist in the season's **cheapest repeatable task** |
| Likely rows | 3.1, 3.3, 3.5, 3.11, S1, S2, S3 | 3.4, 3.6, 3.8, 3.9, 3.10a, S5 |
| **Primary award** | **Control** (if the build is sensor-led) or **Innovate (RTX)** (if it is mechanism-led) | **Design** or **Think** |
| **Hedge** | Innovate or Design | Think or Design (whichever is not primary) |
| Packages | M3 or M2 + M4 + M5 + M6 | M1 or M4 + M5 + M6 |
| Portfolio emphasis | Control block diagram + Table 6-8 component table + development path | Engineering-journey narrative, decision matrix, math page, readability (Table 6-3 E2) |
| Why | Puts the robot-heavy team where machine criteria live | Puts the developing team where fabrication capability is irrelevant and where the BIOBUZZ rewrite **lowered** the bar (Think now needs only one of four options) |

## 6.3 Keeping the two stories distinct [J]

**[C] There is no rule against similar portfolios (A201 sets format limits only), and FIRST's judging guidance
treats sibling teams separately for all awards. The risk is not a violation — it is a scoring one.** Two
near-identical portfolios do not double your odds; they halve the distinctiveness of both.

| Rule | Practice |
|---|---|
| **Different primary awards, always** | Never let both teams walk into the same event arguing Control. Assign primaries at the start of the season and hold them |
| **Same fact, different side of it** | The A/B structure is legitimately Sustain E3 (leadership pipeline) and Reach R2 (a new team recruited). The A team tells it as *"we built the pipeline"*; the B team tells it as *"we are what the pipeline produced."* Same truth, two distinct narratives |
| **Different mechanisms in the Innovate/Design claim** | If both robots share an intake CAD (legal under **R101***'s note, which permits assistance from other teams including contributed COMPONENTS and MECHANISMS, provided each team builds its own MAJOR MECHANISMS — see `research/TWO-TEAM-PROGRAM-RULES.md` §3.1), only one team may claim it as its creative mechanism. The other claims something else, or claims the *whole-robot* Design case instead |
| **Different students speaking** | A judge who sees the same three students in both interviews sees one team with two numbers. Every student presents for exactly one team |
| **Shared engineering, separate authorship** | Share the CAD, the codebase and the chassis. Do **not** share portfolio pages verbatim — each team writes its own version of the shared work, from its own perspective, with its own numbers |
| **Sequence the events if you can** | If both teams cannot attend a mid-size event, prefer sending them where the Table 6-1 pool is deep enough for two slots |

# 8. Anti-patterns

## 8.1 Chasing an award the robot's story does not support

**The failure.** A team decides in September that it wants Innovate, then spends the season searching for
something to call creative. **[C] Innovate R3 is Required and is a hard gate**: the creative design "must be
stable and reliable" and help reach game goals "most of the time." A mechanism that is unusual but flaky loses
the award *and* burns the hours that would have won Design or Control.

**The tell.** The award was chosen before the robot. The mechanism cannot be demonstrated in the pit.

**The fix.** Choose the archetype on merit (`ACHIEVABILITY-FACTORS.md`), then read the pairing off §2.3. The
pairing is a **consequence** of the design, never an input to it. If the pairing you get is Design + Think
rather than the glamorous one, that is the correct answer — and Design explicitly rewards restraint (R1:
"elegant, efficient (simple to build and operate), and/or practical to maintain").

## 8.2 Retrofitting evidence after the fact

**The failure.** In February the team writes a decision matrix for a decision it made in October by feel,
photographs a prototype it rebuilt for the photo, and invents a success rate.

**Why it fails.** **[C] A201.E** requires content dated from 2026-01-01, and judges ask follow-up questions:
*what was the second option, what did it measure, who ran the test, on what date.* A retrofitted matrix has no
losing option with a real number attached, and it shows within two questions. It also costs the team the one
thing it actually had — a truthful record.

**The tell.** Every prototype photo has the same background and the same date. The rejected option is
implausibly bad. Nobody can say who ran a test.

**The fix.** §5.1's rituals. The decision file is written **before** the build, not after. The tally sheet
starts the day the mechanism first works. Fifteen minutes at the moment of the work replaces fifteen hours of
reconstruction — and a reconstructed page is worth less than a scruffy real one.

## 8.3 Letting award-chasing distort design decisions that should be made on merit

**The failure.** Adding a sensor that does nothing so the team can claim Control. Building a more complicated
mechanism than the game needs so it looks creative. Keeping a subsystem that should be cut because a page is
already written about it.

**Why it fails.** **[C] Control R2** requires feedback that "improve[s] how it performs" — a decorative sensor
fails the criterion the moment a judge asks what breaks without it. **[C] Design R1** penalises exactly the
complexity that award-chasing adds. And every hour spent on award theatre is an hour not spent on cycle time,
which is what actually wins matches and alliance selections.

**The tell.** A team can name the award a component is for but not the failure it prevents.

**The fix.** One test before adding anything: *"If we removed this, what measurably gets worse?"* If the only
answer is "our award case," remove it. The strongest award evidence is a genuine engineering decision written
down honestly — which is what all three MCI criteria and Think R1 are actually asking for.

## 8.4 The smaller anti-patterns, in one table

| Anti-pattern | Why it costs you | Rule |
